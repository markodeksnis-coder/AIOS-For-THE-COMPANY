## Overview

Most reps who follow up are actually chasing. The difference sounds subtle but the effect is
significant. Chasing is self-serving: the rep is contacting the prospect because they want an
answer, because their pipeline needs updating, because the month is ending. The prospect
senses this. It creates discomfort, then avoidance.

Follow-up, properly understood, is an act of guidance. You are not contacting the prospect for
your benefit. You are helping them get to a decision — providing structure for a process that
would otherwise drift. That reframe changes the tone of every message you send.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Articulate the difference between chasing and guiding a prospect to a decision.
- Identify the signals that distinguish purposeful follow-up from self-serving contact.
- Reframe your internal motivation for follow-up so that it comes across differently.
- Understand why chasing consistently repels prospects while guiding does not.
- Apply the guidance mindset to every follow-up situation.

## Theory

### The Self-Serving Follow-Up

When a rep follows up because they need an answer, it is almost always perceptible. The
language tends to be vague: "Just checking in," "Wanted to follow up," "Wondering if you'd had a
chance to think it over." These phrases communicate nothing of value to the prospect. They
communicate only that the rep wants something from them.

The prospect's response to this is predictable. They ignore it. Not because they are rude, but
because engaging with it requires effort and offers nothing in return. The rep has given them no
reason to respond.

### Follow-Up as Decision Guidance

Genuine follow-up is oriented toward the prospect's decision, not the rep's pipeline. It asks:
"What does this prospect need in order to get to a decision?" Sometimes that is information.
Sometimes it is a reminder of the problem they described. Sometimes it is a prompt to loop in the
stakeholder they mentioned.

The rep who follows up with "I have been thinking about what you described and wanted to
share one thing that might be useful as you work through the decision" is offering something.
The prospect who receives that message has a reason to engage. The tone is different because
the intent is different.

> The test for any follow-up message: if the prospect never buys, does this message still provide
> them with something of value? If the answer is yes, it is a follow-up. If the answer is no, it is
> chasing.

### The Mindset Shift

The mindset shift from chasing to guiding is not just about language. It requires genuinely
believing that your role is to help the prospect make the right decision — which might be to buy,
but might also be to realise the timing is not right. Reps who hold this frame send better
messages and close more deals, because their follow-up is coherent with the way they ran the
original call.

## Application

### Before sending any follow-up, ask the decision test

Write the message. Then ask: if this prospect never buys, does this message still offer them
something? If it does not, rewrite it until it does. This constraint forces you out of self-serving
language and into genuine guidance.

### Reference the call, not your need

"When we spoke, you mentioned [specific thing]. I wanted to follow up on that specifically..."
This immediately signals to the prospect that you were listening and that you are thinking about
their situation, not your commission.

### Lead with something useful

A relevant article, a question that advances the prospect's thinking, a specific insight from the
call: anything that gives the prospect a reason to engage independently of whether they are
going to buy. Reps who do this build a reputation as someone worth responding to.

## Key Takeaways

- Chasing is self-serving contact. Guiding is oriented toward the prospect's decision.
- Vague language — "just checking in" — signals that the rep wants something without offering
  anything.
- Test every follow-up: if they never buy, does this message still offer them value?
- Reference what they said on the call — it proves you were listening.
- The mindset shift from chasing to guiding changes the tone of every message you send.
