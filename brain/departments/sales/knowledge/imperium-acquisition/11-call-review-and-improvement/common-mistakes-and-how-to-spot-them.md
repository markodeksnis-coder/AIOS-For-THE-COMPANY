## Overview

Every rep has patterns — repeated behaviours that surface across calls
regardless of the prospect, the product, or the stage in the process.
Most of these patterns are invisible to the rep because they feel
natural. You do not notice talking too much if talking too much is what
you have always done.

This lesson identifies the four most common call mistakes, shows you
what they sound like on a recording, and gives you the self-diagnosis
method for spotting them in your own calls. Recognising the pattern is
always the first step to fixing it.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Identify the four most common call mistakes in your own recordings.
- Diagnose which mistakes apply to your current call pattern.
- Understand what each mistake sounds like and why it happens.
- Begin correcting each mistake through targeted awareness and practice.
- Build self-awareness around your personal mistake pattern over time.

## Theory

### Mistake One: Talking Too Much

**The sign:** the prospect is speaking for less than 50% of the call. On
a recording, it sounds like the rep filling silences, jumping to
features before the prospect has finished answering a question, or
providing context the prospect did not ask for. **The cause** is usually
anxiety or lack of confidence in the discovery process. **The fix:**
count your questions. A rep who is asking good questions is not talking
too much.

### Mistake Two: Weak Discovery

**The sign:** the call moves from situation questions to pitch in under
ten minutes, without any implication or urgency questions. On a
recording, it sounds like a list of surface facts rather than a
developing picture of a specific problem. **The cause** is usually
discomfort with staying in discovery mode. **The fix:** make it a rule
not to pitch until you can answer "what is this costing them,
specifically?"

### Mistake Three: Rushing the Pitch

**The sign:** the pitch arrives before the confirmation frame, or the
diagnosis summary was omitted. On a recording, the pitch lands without
setup — it appears abruptly after discovery without the transition that
makes it feel earned. **The cause** is usually the rep's eagerness to
get to the solution. **The fix:** never pitch without the confirmation
frame. It is a mandatory checkpoint.

> A rushed pitch does not just land poorly. It signals to the prospect
> that you did not fully hear them — that you were waiting to pitch
> rather than listening to understand. That signal erodes the trust
> built in discovery faster than almost anything else.

### Mistake Four: Avoiding the Close

**The sign:** the call ends without a direct close question — with a
plan to "follow up next week" rather than an actual decision. On a
recording, the rep's energy drops after the pitch, the language becomes
vague, and the close question is either not asked or asked in a form so
indirect it cannot produce a yes or a no. **The cause** is fear of
rejection. **The fix:** commit to the direct close question on every
call, regardless of how the pitch landed.

## Application

### Diagnose your current pattern

Listen to your last five calls and check for each of the four mistakes.
Which ones appear consistently? Which are absent? Build a simple
pattern: "My most consistent mistake is [X], and it typically shows up
at [stage] of the call."

### Create a pre-call checklist

For your top one or two mistakes, create a pre-call reminder. A single
sentence that you read before each call: "This call: ask at least one
implication question before moving on from each problem area." Over
time, the habit becomes internal.

### Listen specifically for your mistake on the next call

On your next recording, do not review the whole call. Listen
specifically for the mistake you are working on. Did it appear? When?
What triggered it? That level of specificity is what produces correction
rather than general awareness.

## Key Takeaways

- The four common mistakes: talking too much, weak discovery, rushing
  the pitch, avoiding the close.
- Each mistake has a recognisable sound on a recording — train your ear
  to hear them.
- Each mistake has a root cause — usually anxiety, discomfort, or habit.
- Diagnose your personal pattern before attempting to fix anything.
- Targeted listening on recordings beats general review every time.
