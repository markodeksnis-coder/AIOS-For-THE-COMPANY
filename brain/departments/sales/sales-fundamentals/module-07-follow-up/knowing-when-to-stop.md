## Overview

One of the hardest skills in follow-up is knowing when to stop. Reps who follow up too many
times damage relationships, develop a reputation for being pushy, and spend time on deals that
are not going to close. Reps who stop too early leave money on the table. Finding the right point
requires both a framework and honest self-assessment.

This lesson gives you the signals that tell you a lead is genuinely gone, a rule of thumb for how
many follow-ups is appropriate, and the language for a final message that closes the loop cleanly
and preserves the relationship for the future.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Identify the signals that distinguish a dead lead from a cold one.
- Apply a consistent rule for how many follow-ups to send before stopping.
- Write a final message that closes the loop respectfully and preserves the relationship.
- Understand why letting go of dead leads improves overall pipeline performance.
- Stop making follow-up decisions based on hope rather than evidence.

## Theory

### The Signals That a Lead Is Gone

The clearest signal is specificity — or the absence of it. A prospect who is genuinely interested
but delayed will usually give you specific information when pushed: a timeline, a condition, a
reason for the delay that makes sense. A prospect who has decided not to move forward will give
you vague, non-committal responses or no response at all.

Other signals: multiple follow-ups with no response of any kind; a prospect who has stopped
opening your emails (if you can track this); a prospect who explicitly said they would be in touch
by a date that has passed without any communication.

### The Rule of Thumb

There is no universally correct number of follow-ups. But a reasonable framework for most
situations is: two to three purposeful follow-ups after the initial call, spaced appropriately,
followed by one re-engagement message after a longer gap, followed by one final message if
there is still no response. Total: four to five contacts after the call. After that, the lead should be
parked or closed, depending on whether there is a realistic future window.

The key word is purposeful. Each message should have a specific reason for being sent. If you
cannot articulate why you are sending this message, specifically, at this moment, it is probably
chasing rather than following up.

> Letting go of dead leads is not giving up. It is focusing your time on the prospects where
> genuine progress is possible. A pipeline full of leads that are not going to close is a pipeline
> that misleads you about your true performance and fills your calendar with unproductive
> activity.

### The Final Message

> "I've reached out a few times since we spoke and have not heard back — I do not want to keep
> contacting you if the timing is not right. I'll leave it here for now, but if circumstances change
> and [specific problem] becomes a priority again, the conversation is always open." Clean,
> respectful, no pressure. This message does two things: it closes the loop, and it leaves the
> door open cleanly. A surprisingly large number of reps who send this message get a response.

## Application

### Define your follow-up sequence in advance

For your typical deal type, decide: how many follow-ups, over what timeframe, with what
purpose at each stage. Write this down. Having a defined sequence means you are not making
emotional decisions about whether to follow up — you are following a system.

### Set a date for the final message

When you add a lead to follow-up, set a date by which, if there has been no response, you will
send the final message. This prevents deals from lingering indefinitely in your pipeline without a
decision.

### Review your pipeline for leads that should be closed

Once a month, review every lead in your pipeline that is more than 60 days old with no
meaningful progress. For each one, make a decision: is there a genuine reason to continue, or is
this wishful thinking? Purging dead leads from your pipeline is one of the most reliable ways to
improve your sense of where your real opportunities are.

## Key Takeaways

- The signal a lead is gone: vague or no responses after multiple specific attempts.
- Rule of thumb: two to three follow-ups, a re-engagement, then a final message — then stop.
- Every follow-up must have a specific, articulable reason. If it does not, it is chasing.
- The final message closes the loop cleanly and often produces a response.
- Letting go of dead leads is not giving up — it is focusing time on real opportunities.
