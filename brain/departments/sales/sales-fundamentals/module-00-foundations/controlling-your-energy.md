Module 0, Section 6 of the Sales Fundamentals & Control curriculum.

## Overview

Some reps walk out of bad calls and say the prospect was difficult. The reps who close at a high
rate walk out of the same calls having stayed grounded and kept things moving. The difference is
rarely skill level in the traditional sense. It is energy management: the ability to show up in the
right state, stay calibrated during the call, and not let a curveball or a tough moment throw you
off your game.

This lesson covers why some reps sound like salespeople and others do not, how to read and
calibrate to the prospect's energy, what to do when a call goes off track, and how to build a
pre-call routine that gives you a genuine edge before you even join the meeting.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Understand why some reps trigger a "salesperson" response in prospects and how to avoid
  it.
- Read the prospect's energy and calibrate your own approach in response.
- Stay grounded and composed when a call goes off track or a prospect pushes back hard.
- Build a repeatable pre-call routine that puts you in the right headspace.
- Understand the difference between high energy and grounded certainty, and why only one of
  them closes deals.

## Theory

### Why Some Reps Sound Like Salespeople

There is a specific energy pattern that triggers what psychologists call the "sales resistance"
response in prospects. It is not about the words. It is about the neediness behind them. When a
rep is over-enthusiastic, uses high-energy openers designed to build false rapport, or displays
the kind of relentless positivity that feels performative, the prospect's defences activate
immediately.

This is because human beings are highly attuned to inauthenticity. We detect it in
micro-expressions, vocal tone, and energy long before we can articulate what felt off. A rep who
sounds like they are "on" in a way that is clearly performance-driven creates distance, not
connection. The antidote is not lower energy. It is authentic, grounded presence.

### Emotional Contagion and Calibration

The concept of emotional contagion (Hatfield et al., 1993) describes the unconscious
synchronisation of emotional states between people. In practice, this means that your energy
state on a call directly influences the prospect's emotional experience of the conversation.

The best reps use this consciously. If a prospect is guarded and quiet, matching their energy at a
slightly warmer level creates safety rather than pressure. If a prospect is energised and talkative,
meeting that with composed engagement rather than matching their peak energy keeps you in
the guide role. The goal is always to lead the energy slightly, not follow it reactively or overpower
it.

> Calibration is not about being a chameleon. It is about being perceptive. You notice where the
> prospect is, you meet them close to that point, and then gradually you lead the conversation to
> where it needs to go. This is what it feels like to be guided rather than sold to.

### Staying Grounded When Calls Go Off Track

Every rep has calls that go sideways. The prospect challenges you early, the conversation takes
an unexpected turn, there is a long silence that catches you off guard, or someone says
something that throws your frame. What separates high-performing reps from average ones in
these moments is not that they have a scripted response ready. It is that they have the
groundedness to pause, recalibrate, and continue without spiralling.

This groundedness is trainable. It is built through deliberate exposure to difficult scenarios,
through the pre-call routine that sets a stable baseline, and through the understanding that a
tough moment on a call does not mean the call is lost. It means you have a moment to navigate.

## Application

### Two minutes of stillness before you join

Not scrolling, not reviewing notes, not rushing from another call. Two minutes of deliberate
stillness. Breathe slowly. Check your posture. Let your nervous system settle. This is not
performance preparation. It is a simple physiological reset that genuinely changes how you enter
the conversation.

### Review the prospect, not your pitch

In the final few minutes before a call, focus on what you know about the prospect's situation, not
on what you are going to say. Shift your orientation from "what am I going to cover" to "what is
going on for this person". It changes your energy from presenter to genuinely curious guide
before you have even said a word.

### When the call goes off track

If something unexpected happens, acknowledge it briefly and redirect. "That's a fair point. Let
me make sure I address that properly. Before I do, can I ask..." gives you a moment to collect
yourself and keeps the structure intact. The prospect rarely notices the reset. They notice the
composure.

### After a bad call

Do not let a difficult call contaminate the next one. Give yourself two minutes to note what
happened, identify the earliest point where things went off course, and then actively reset before
the next call begins. Carrying the energy from a bad call into a new one is one of the most
common and most preventable reasons for consecutive losses.

> You cannot fake groundedness for long. But you can build it. Reps who show up consistently in
> the right state are not naturally calmer people. They have built routines that create the
> conditions for composure. That is a skill, and it is completely learnable.

## Key Takeaways

- Reps sound like salespeople because of performative energy, not because of their words.
- Emotional contagion means your state directly shapes the prospect's experience of the call.
- Calibrate to the prospect's energy, then lead it — do not match it or overpower it.
- Groundedness in difficult moments comes from routine and practice, not natural temperament.
- A pre-call reset is not optional. It is a performance tool.
